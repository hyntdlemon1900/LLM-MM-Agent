The core methodology adopted for this problem is a hybrid heuristic-decomposition approach that combines a greedy initialization with a local search refinement phase, designed specifically to handle the high-dimensional combinatorial nature of the joint placement and routing problem under strict resource constraints. This choice is motivated by the fact that solving the full Mixed-Integer Linear Program (MILP) exactly becomes intractable for large-scale modern clusters due to the exponential growth in variables and constraints with the number of jobs, workers, and switches. Instead, we leverage the structural properties of the problem—such as the single-hop INA constraint, the clear separation between placement and routing decisions, and the path-based load definitions—to design a scalable algorithm that approximates the optimal solution while guaranteeing feasibility. The strategy centers on a two-stage process: first, a greedy placement phase that selects K switches based on a novel "aggregation impact" metric, which evaluates each switch’s potential to reduce job completion times by aggregating high-volume or high-rate workers; second, a constrained routing phase that assigns each worker to either the selected INA switches or the PS using a rate-aware, load-balancing assignment rule, followed by a local improvement loop that iteratively adjusts assignments and placements to further reduce the makespan. The algorithm ensures feasibility through careful constraint enforcement at every stage: placement respects the budget K, each worker is assigned to exactly one aggregation point (either an INA switch or the PS), and all switch processing and link bandwidth limits are checked and enforced via explicit load calculation using precomputed paths. To manage the coupling between binary decisions and continuous rates, we use big-M logic implicitly by only allowing rate flows when the corresponding assignment variable is active, and we enforce the per-job rate consistency by ensuring that the job’s effective rate γ_j is bounded by the minimum of all worker sending rates, thus capturing the bottleneck effect. The key innovation lies in the design of the placement heuristic, which computes a dynamic score for each candidate switch by estimating its contribution to reducing the slowest job’s completion time, based on the total volume and rate of workers that could be routed through it, weighted by the expected path congestion and PS proximity. This score is updated in real-time during the local search to reflect the current assignment state, enabling the algorithm to avoid poor placements that would create hotspots or underutilized INA switches. During routing, we employ a greedy assignment with fairness constraints: for each worker, we evaluate all valid aggregation points (selected INA switches and the PS), compute the resulting link and switch loads, and choose the option that minimizes the maximum link utilization or maximizes the aggregate throughput, while respecting the big-M constraints and ensuring that the total worker rate does not exceed the switch’s processing capacity. The local search phase introduces small perturbations—such as swapping a worker’s assignment between an INA switch and the PS, or swapping two INA placements—to explore the neighborhood of the current solution and escape local optima. This process continues until no further improvements in α (the inverse makespan) are observed or a maximum iteration count is reached. The algorithm is inherently scalable because the greedy placement and routing steps operate in near-linear time with respect to the number of workers and switches, and the local search is limited to a small number of iterations, making the overall complexity manageable even for thousands of workers and hundreds of switches. Robustness is ensured through fallback mechanisms: if no valid assignment exists during routing, the algorithm falls back to a default assignment (e.g., direct routing to PS), and the placement phase includes a randomized restart option to avoid poor initialization. By combining structural insight with practical heuristics, the solution effectively balances optimality and efficiency, delivering near-optimal makespan reductions compared to baseline strategies, while maintaining strict adherence to all system constraints, including bandwidth limits, INA capacity, single-hop routing, and the binary placement budget. This approach is particularly well-suited for real-world deployment in dynamic machine learning clusters where rapid, repeatable decisions are needed at every training iteration.
